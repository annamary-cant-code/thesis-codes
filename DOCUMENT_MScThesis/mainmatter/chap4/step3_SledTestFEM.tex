\section{Step 3 - Sled test (FEM)}\label{section:step3-sledTest}
The scope of this third step within the workflow is to subject the payload bay, i.e. the container of the medical payload analysed until now, to the type of acceleration it would possibly undergo in an impact event. Specifically, the idea is to imitate a \textit{sled test}, which is common practice in the automotive industry: an ATD\footnote{\textit{Anthropomorphic Test Devices}.} crash dummy is strapped onto a sled (a trolley sliding on almost frictionless rails) that is subjected to a prescribed acceleration pulse, and the response of the dummies, governed predominantly by their own inertia and the forces exerted by the restraint system, is checked against damage thresholds \hl{cite that paper with the sled test and dummy damage indices!!!!} 

The model built in this Step 3 aims to simulate a similar phenomenon: here, an acceleration boundary condition is imposed on the external shell of the medical payload container, leaving its contents to be governed by their inertial response and by the contact forces exchanged between each other. The model aims to verify whether the imposed acceleration impulse damages the medical payload inside, i.e. the glass phials. The outcome is, therefore, binary: either the phials are damaged, or they are not. The information related to crack initiation and material failure is monitored through the same LS-DYNA variable activated in Step 2 to evaluate phial failure load under axial compression. The apparition of damage implies that the acceleration pulse to which the container was subjected was too high for the phials to resist and the \textit{allowable} (i.e. maximum withstandable) peak acceleration must correspond to a lower value. The test is in itself can be reduced to a threshold problem, where exceeding a certain peak acceleration immediately induces damage (with due considerations on tolerance). Finding the acceleration allowable $a_{allow}$ is an iterative process and results in obtaining the last input necessary for the airbag design optimisation code of the workflow's Step 4.

Just like the FEM model for the axial compression test, the present model is built using \textit{explicit} time integration with an engineering unit system consistent with the one adopted in Step 2, reported here again for clarity:

The input of the model is an acceleration time history, prescribed through \kw{BOUNDARY\_PRESCRIBED\_MOTION\_RIGID} on two rigid rings attached to the payload bay [how are they connected? shared nodes? constrained nodes?], while the rest of the bay remains deformable. The rings represent the straps that hold the payload, in the same way as the restraints on a test sled. The curve has a trapezoidal shape: an initial ramp of duration $t_r$, a plateau at constant acceleration $a$ of duration $t_p$, and a descending ramp of duration $t_r$. The ramps avoid discontinuities in the acceleration, which would excite spurious high-frequency oscillations, so that the velocity and displacement histories remain smooth. The acceleration is therefore applied at the attachment points, and reaches the phials only after being transmitted through the deformable bay, the sponge and the mask.

The impact is characterised by an initial velocity $\mathbf{u}_0 = (u_{x,0}, u_{y,0})$, which includes both horizontal and vertical components [how are the $x$, $y$, $z$ components handled?]. Over the deceleration this velocity must be brought completely to zero, so the integral of the acceleration curve must equal the velocity dissipated by the system:
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^{T} a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = a\,(t_r + t_p) = \lVert \mathbf{u}_0 \rVert,
  \qquad T = 2t_r + t_p .
  \label{eq:pulse_area}
\end{equation}
This condition defines a family of acceleration curves: for a given amplitude $a$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pulse_area} fixes the plateau duration $t_p = \lVert \mathbf{u}_0 \rVert / a - t_r$, and therefore the characteristic time scale of the event. The aim is to reproduce an impact as closely as possible, applying an acceleration profile similar to the peak observed in an impact event.

The maximum acceleration the phials can withstand is found with a search procedure analogous to the calibration of \texttt{FT}: the amplitude $a$ is increased step by step, with the duration adjusted through Eq.~\eqref{eq:pulse_area}, until the phial breaks. Since a larger amplitude also implies a shorter pulse, the resulting limit refers to this pulse shape and to the given initial velocity.

The limit is expressed in terms of the acceleration prescribed at the rings [or: measured at ...?].

\subsubsection{Requested outputs and failure detection}
% how "phial broken" is defined: first crack (HISV#1), force/energy drop, element deletion
% NODOUT (phial node acc.), RCFORC, GLSTAT, MATSUM, d3plot HISV
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Definition of ``broken''.} State which event counts as failure: first crack initiation at any integration point (\texttt{HISV\#1} $\geq 1$), or through-thickness failure (all \texttt{NIPF} $= 5$ points cracked).
    \item \textbf{Consistency with Step 2.} The failure event must be the same one used to read the failure force in the \texttt{FT} calibration (first force drop). If the definitions differ, \texttt{FT} and the allowable refer to different events.
    \item \textbf{Primary indicator.} \texttt{HISV\#1} crack flag from d3plot, using the max-IP fringe.
    \item \textbf{Secondary confirmation.} Phial internal-energy discontinuity (\texttt{MATSUM}), plus the jump in the phial node's acceleration (\texttt{NODOUT}).
    \item \textbf{What is not a criterion.} \texttt{EPSCR} $= 0.1$ deletion is a numerical backstop, not a failure definition.
    \item \textbf{Spurious events excluded.} \texttt{HISV\#1} $= -1$ (compressive flag), and contact-penetration spikes.
    \item \textbf{Time resolution.} The output interval has to be fine enough to place the crack within the acceleration plateau.
    \item \textbf{Allowable definition.} The highest acceleration level without failure, i.e.\ the bracket between the last pass and the first fail, with the sweep step stated as the resolution.
\end{itemize}
