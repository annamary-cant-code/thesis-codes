\subsubsection{Loading}
% characteristic time scale calculated based on the integral of acceleration being the dissipated velocity
% *BOUNDARY_PRESCRIBED_MOTION_RIGID on the bay, acceleration curve (shape, rise, plateau)
% amplitude search: increase a until failure (analogue of FT calibration procedure)
The input of the model is an acceleration time history, prescribed through \texttt{*BOUNDARY\_PRESCRIBED\_MOTION\_RIGID} on two rigid rings attached to the payload bay [how are they connected? shared nodes? constrained nodes?], while the rest of the bay remains deformable. The rings represent the straps that hold the payload, in the same way as the restraints on a test sled. The curve has a trapezoidal shape: an initial ramp of duration $t_r$, a plateau at constant acceleration $a$ of duration $t_p$, and a descending ramp of duration $t_r$. The ramps avoid discontinuities in the acceleration, which would excite spurious high-frequency oscillations, so that the velocity and displacement histories remain smooth. The acceleration is therefore applied at the attachment points, and reaches the phials only after being transmitted through the deformable bay, the sponge and the mask.

The impact is characterised by an initial velocity $\mathbf{v}_0$, which includes both horizontal and vertical components [how are the $x$, $y$, $z$ components handled?]. Over the deceleration this velocity must be brought completely to zero, so the integral of the acceleration curve must equal the velocity dissipated by the system:
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^{T} a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = a\,(t_r + t_p) = \lVert \mathbf{v}_0 \rVert,
  \qquad T = 2t_r + t_p .
  \label{eq:pulse_area}
\end{equation}
This condition defines a family of acceleration curves: for a given amplitude $a$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pulse_area} fixes the plateau duration $t_p = \lVert \mathbf{v}_0 \rVert / a - t_r$, and therefore the characteristic time scale of the event. The aim is to reproduce an impact as closely as possible, applying an acceleration profile similar to the peak observed in an impact event.

The maximum acceleration the phials can withstand is found with a search procedure analogous to the calibration of \texttt{FT}: the amplitude $a$ is increased step by step, with the duration adjusted through Eq.~\eqref{eq:pulse_area}, until the phial breaks. Since a larger amplitude also implies a shorter pulse, the resulting limit refers to this pulse shape and to the given initial velocity.

The limit is expressed in terms of the acceleration prescribed at the rings [or: measured at ...?].
Acceleration applied in the x, y, and z direction, and sweeping to detect the most critical direction.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Why it's needed.} The 0.1~mm phial shells set the stable timestep ($\Delta t \approx l_e/c \approx 1.9\times10^{-5}$~ms). Multiply that by the event duration to show the cycle count is impractical without scaling, even on DelftBlue.
    \item \textbf{How it's applied.} \texttt{*CONTROL\_TIMESTEP}, \texttt{DT2MS}: give the target $\Delta t$, and say whether scaling applies only to elements below the target (negative \texttt{DT2MS}).
    \item \textbf{Where the mass lands.} Almost entirely on the phial, since it has the smallest elements. Report it as numbers: phial 3~g $\rightarrow$ 170~g ($\times 57$), and the added mass as a \% of the total model.
    \item \textbf{Physical consequence.} At a given bay acceleration, the phial's inertial load scales with its mass. The phial--sponge frequency drops by $\sqrt{57} \approx 7.5\times$.
    \item \textbf{How the bias is handled.} Either a native-mass reference run, or the analytical SDOF correction with its validity condition stated (deep isolation, $r \gg 1$, including the native case).
    \item \textbf{Check.} Added mass reported from \texttt{d3hsp}/\texttt{GLSTAT}; KE/IE stays low during the plateau.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Requested outputs and failure detection}
% how "phial broken" is defined: first crack (HISV#1), force/energy drop, element deletion
% NODOUT (phial node acc.), RCFORC, GLSTAT, MATSUM, d3plot HISV
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Definition of ``broken''.} State which event counts as failure: first crack initiation at any integration point (\texttt{HISV\#1} $\geq 1$), or through-thickness failure (all \texttt{NIPF} $= 5$ points cracked).
    \item \textbf{Consistency with Step 2.} The failure event must be the same one used to read the failure force in the \texttt{FT} calibration (first force drop). If the definitions differ, \texttt{FT} and the allowable refer to different events.
    \item \textbf{Primary indicator.} \texttt{HISV\#1} crack flag from d3plot, using the max-IP fringe.
    \item \textbf{Secondary confirmation.} Phial internal-energy discontinuity (\texttt{MATSUM}), plus the jump in the phial node's acceleration (\texttt{NODOUT}).
    \item \textbf{What is not a criterion.} \texttt{EPSCR} $= 0.1$ deletion is a numerical backstop, not a failure definition.
    \item \textbf{Spurious events excluded.} \texttt{HISV\#1} $= -1$ (compressive flag), and contact-penetration spikes.
    \item \textbf{Time resolution.} The output interval has to be fine enough to place the crack within the acceleration plateau.
    \item \textbf{Allowable definition.} The highest acceleration level without failure, i.e.\ the bracket between the last pass and the first fail, with the sweep step stated as the resolution.
\end{itemize}
