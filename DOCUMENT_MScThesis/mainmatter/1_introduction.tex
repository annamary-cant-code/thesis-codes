\section{Motivation}
The present MSc Thesis in Aerospace Structures and Materials falls under the category of \textit{Engineering Design}, in alternative to the \textit{Scientific Research}-type projects. In \textit{design} projects, it is required to attempt the process of innovation not through the discovery and exploration of a research niche, but rather by applying pre-existing scientific knowledge to enable the concrete realisation of a technical design. In the case of the present thesis, the overall objective consists in proposing a \textit{deisign workflow} and validating it through testing and simulation. If adopted in the preliminary design phase of a drone-based delivery mission, this workflow yields an optimised airbag-type device tailored to a specific failure-and-drop application, in line with the requirements set by the project commissioners. Therefore, the successful completion of this thesis is achieved by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Proposing an application-specific design workflow that fits within the broader context of the ongoing research effort;
    \item Testing the workflow, both experimentally and through simulation, to realistically inform the device's design. 
\end{enumerate} 

The design workflow (informally called \textit{inner (design) loop} throughout this thesis) is inteded as a first attempt to bridge the gap between the long-term, broadly-defined objective of constructing a large-scale drone network (also called \textit{outer (design) loop}) over the region of Piedmont, Italy, and the previously completed research work, consisting in a series of logistic, legislative, and generally high-level evaluations of possible \textbf{drone-based missions} with the objective to \textbf{deliver medical supplies in emergency scenarios}. Such work was conducted by Prof. Enrico Cestino's research group at \textit{Politecnico di Torino}, in collaboration with the \textit{A.O.U. Città della Salute e della Scienza di Torino} hospital complex and automotive consulting company \textit{FEV Italia S.r.l.}, three of the main industrial and research stakeholders working towards helping hospitals in the transition from road to air transport to enhance the logistics of the \textit{Sistema Sanitario Nazionale} (\textit{SSN}, the Italian National Healthcare System) both at an intra- and inter-hospital level.  

This MSc Thesis Project is, therefore, a multi-disciplinary project where the knowledge and techniques of Structural Engineering are put to the service of the Medical field. The airbag-like device resulting from the design process should be able to protect the free-falling medical payload in the event of a drone failure and its subsequent detachment from the latter. The device aims to bring the impact accelerations within the allowable limits of the payload container and of the medical payload being transported, in compliance with the safety regulations regarding dispersion of chemical substances into the environment. However, contrary to most aeronautical projects which do not distinguish between the integrity of the aerial vehicle and the safety of its passengers, this project adopts a "cargo-first" approach where the protection of the highly valuable medical payload is prioritised over the integrity of the drone carrying it. Distinguishing between the safety aspects of the UAV and its payload implies a certain facilitation in the certification process of the UAV, as well as for the modular implementation of the safety device on a variety of drones serving in different, specific delivery missions.

\subsection{PoliTo’s research group’s work (including normative) and expected contribution}
* Iniziativa dell'A.o.U. città della salute e della scienza di Torino, in collaborazione con il Politecnico di Torino e in occasione del presente lavoro di tesi anche con FEV Italia S.r.l, ha preso l'iniziativa di ricerca per tentare di risolvere il problema, iniziando una serie di progetti a catena con una visione a lungo termine: snellire i l trasporto logistico all'interno della sanità implementando un sistema di droni, che possano aiutare con vari scenari. 

Knowing that the long-term objective is the drone network, this is the work that this particular research group has done until now. The 5 MSc theses, the scientific works, etc. My contribution: an answer to PoliTo’s research: formalise (at a high level, non-detailed level) the project and take a little step forward, toward the long term objective, deciding on the approach. 

In recent years, \textit{Politecnico di Torino}, \textit{ASL Città di Torino}, and \textit{A.O.U. Città della Salute e della Scienza di Torino} have launched a strategic research initiative focused on the use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for the delivery of critical medical goods across Torino and its province. The long-term vision is the creation of a fully operational drone-based logistics network capable of rapidly and safely transporting medical supplies, thereby improving delivery speed, efficiency, operational capacity (especially payload mass), and overall safety.

To date, in addition to several publications (e.g. Angelini (2024) \cite{Angelini2024ICAS}), the research effort has produced five Master’s theses, each addressing key aspects required to establish a complete and regulatory-compliant UAV medical delivery ecosystem:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cavallo (2020)} \cite{Cavallo2020}: Preliminary assessment of UAV platforms suitable under Italian and European regulations. Development of risk maps and definition of optimal trajectories between selected hospitals.
    \item \textbf{Bertelli (2021)} \cite{Bertelli2021}: Detailed selection of UAV based on mission specifications derived from the medical goods to be transported and ENAC regulatory constraints. Application of the SORA (Specific Operations Risk Assessment) methodology to three feasible mission routes, yielding the safety requirements that operators must satisfy.
    \item \textbf{Sivieri (2021)} \cite{Sivieri2021}: Identification and characterisation of thermal and vibrational loads encountered during typical UAV medical missions. Experimental testing on milk-derived sera samples to determine their resistance to UAV-induced vibrational stresses.
    \item \textbf{Fiorentino (2021)} \cite{Fiorentino2021}: Development of a path-planning strategy for life-saving drug distribution in populated environments. Generation of risk maps and implementation of an RRT-based algorithm to minimise global risk and flight time.
    \item \textbf{Angelini (2023)} \cite{Angelini2023}: Comparative analysis of the safety and economic efficiency of emergency medical deliveries performed by car, drone, and VTOL aircraft. Identification of optimal pharmaceutical delivery hub locations for each transport mode to maximise logistical performance.
\end{itemize}
