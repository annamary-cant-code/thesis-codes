\section{Motivation}
The present MSc thesis in Aerospace Structures and Materials falls under the category of \textit{Engineering Design}, in alternative to the \textit{Scientific Research}-type projects. In \textit{design} projects, it is required to attempt the process of innovation not through the discovery and exploration of a research niche, but rather by applying pre-existing scientific knowledge to enable the concrete realisation of a technical design. In the case of the present thesis, the overall objective consists in proposing a \textit{design workflow} and demonstrating it through testing and simulation. If adopted in the preliminary design phase of a drone-based delivery mission, this workflow yields an optimised airbag-type device tailored to a specific failure-and-drop application, in line with the requirements set by the project commissioners. Therefore, the successful completion of this thesis is achieved by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Proposing an application-specific design workflow that fits within the broader context of the ongoing research effort;
    \item Testing the workflow, both experimentally and through simulation, to realistically inform the device's design. 
\end{enumerate} 

The design workflow (informally called \textit{inner design loop} throughout this thesis) is intended as a first attempt to bridge the gap between the long-term, broadly-defined objective of constructing a large-scale drone network (also called \textit{outer (design) loop}) over the region of Piedmont, Italy, and the previously completed research work, consisting in a series of logistic, legislative, and generally high-level evaluations of possible \textbf{drone-based missions} with the objective to \textbf{deliver medical supplies in emergency scenarios}. Such work was conducted at \textit{Politecnico di Torino}, in collaboration with the \textit{A.O.U. Città della Salute e della Scienza di Torino} hospital complex and automotive consulting company \textit{FEV Italia S.r.l.}, three of the main industrial and research stakeholders working towards helping hospitals in the transition from road to air transport to enhance the logistics of the Italian National Health Service (SSN, \textit{Servizio Sanitario Nazionale}) both at an intra- and inter-hospital level.  

This MSc thesis project is, therefore, a multidisciplinary project where the knowledge and techniques of Structural Engineering are put to the service of the medical field. The airbag-like device resulting from the design process should be able to protect the free-falling medical payload in the event of a drone failure and its subsequent detachment from the latter. The device aims to bring the impact accelerations within the allowable limits of the payload container and of the medical payload being transported, in compliance with the safety regulations regarding dispersion of chemical substances into the environment. However, contrary to most aeronautical projects which do not distinguish between the integrity of the aerial vehicle and the safety of its passengers, this project adopts a \textit{safety-first} approach where the protection of the highly valuable medical cargo is prioritised over the integrity of the drone carrying it. Distinguishing between the safety aspects of the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) and its payload implies a certain facilitation in the certification process of the UAV, as well as for the modular implementation of the safety device on a variety of UAVs serving in different, specific delivery missions.  


\section{Project framing}
\subsection{Care centralisation and territorial inequity in the Italian healthcare system}\label{subsec:care-centralisation}
The idea of transforming healthcare delivery networks from road- to air-transport-based systems stems from the consensus within the Italian medical field, consolidated during the public health emergency of the COVID-19 pandemic (2020--2022), that the current ambulance-based architecture of the SSN's logistic infrastructure (described in detail\footnote{ \textit{"The type of vehicles used varies from region to region. In Piedmont it mainly consists of ambulances used only for transport plus more advanced vehicles equipped for resuscitation, replaced in some sites by mSa on a car with a doctor, nurse and rescuer driver, organised on the basis of specific needs of the territory, different in the four macro-areas of the emergency network (rEmE) in which the region was divided."}\cite{Fantato2023}} in \cite{Fantato2023}) cannot accommodate the healthcare system's needs and transitions. The two main - and competing - factors are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Care centralisation}: Abrescia et al. \cite{Abrescia2026} describe care centralisation as the ongoing process of concentrating medical expertise and services in key geographical areas, e.g. provincial and/or regional capitals, to meet the demands of high-volume care. The downside of care centralisation is that at a regional level, it induces "distance-related inequity, particularly for rural and peripheral populations", especially heightened in emergency scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Inhomogeneous distribution of emergency services }: Garattini et al. \cite{Garattini2020} affirm that, in contrast with the centralisation manoeuvre described above, emergency resources are still inhomogeneously distributed over the regional territories, with services overloaded in densely populated areas and underused in low-density ones, resulting in inefficient overall utilisation.
\end{enumerate} 

In conclusion, \cite{Capuzzo2022} builds on these two points by affirming the importance of establishing a generally more efficient strategy for healthcare management at all levels, and incentivises the development of technologies suitable for upgrading, among the several aspects of the SSN, the logistics of the Italian healthcare supply chain. 


\subsection{Prior research and positioning of the present thesis}\label{section:prior-research}
Several industry-research partnerships aiming to modernise transportation means within the Italian SSN have ventured into the globally booming market of unmanned aerial vehicles, where remotely-piloted or fully autonomous drones are being used ever more often to carry medical payloads in a plethora of scenarios: intra- vs. inter-hospital delivery \cite{Balasingam2017}, ordinary logistics vs. emergency missions and humanitarian applications\cite{Scott2017}, experimental/direct flight between strategic poles \cite{Odigie2021} vs. a fully-operational, inter-hospital, hub-based UAV network covering national territory (the \textit{Zipline} case study\cite{Gangwal2019}). 

Following the global trend to answer a local need, in the specific case of the Piedmont region in north-west Italy, where at the time of writing there exists no city- or region-wide UAV network deployed for the delivery of medical goods, many pilot programmes (further analysed in \cref{section:market-analysis}) are emerging, among which a partnership between the Pharmacy department of the \textit{A.O.U. Città della Salute e della Scienza di Torino} hospital complex (head-quartered at Molinette Hospital in Turin), a research group within the Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering (DIMEAS, \textit{Dipartimento di Ingegneria Meccanica e Aerospaziale}) at \textit{Politecnico di Torino}, and the Numerical Analysis Team of FEV Italia, the Italian branch of the German-founded multinational company FEV, a global leader in research and development for the automotive industry. 

The six-year-long partnership between these three institutions is the backbone of the present MSc thesis project, the most recent of a chain of research projects aiming to pursue the long-term vision of flanking, if not completely replacing, the current road-based transportation means with aerial alternatives within hospitals in Piedmont and, specifically, in the city of Turin (the region's capital), in favour of more rapid, more efficient, and safer transportation of medical supplies. Specifically, this research effort has broadly tackled the key aspects required to establish a complete and regulation-compliant UAV medical delivery ecosystem in the Piedmontese territory, resulting in five MSc theses and one conference paper presenting the following results:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cavallo (2020)} \cite{Cavallo2020}: preliminary assessment of UAV platforms suitable under Italian and European regulations. Development of risk maps and definition of optimal trajectories between selected hospitals.
    \item \textbf{Bertelli (2021)} \cite{Bertelli2021}: detailed selection of UAV based on mission specifications derived from the medical goods to be transported and regulatory constraints set by the Italian Civil Aviation Authority (ENAC, \textit{Ente Nazionale per l'Aviazione Civile}). Application of the SORA (Specific Operations Risk Assessment) methodology to three feasible mission routes, yielding the safety requirements that operators must satisfy.
    \item \textbf{Sivieri (2021)} \cite{Sivieri2021}: identification and characterisation of thermal and vibrational loads encountered during typical UAV medical missions. Experimental testing on milk-derived sera samples to determine their resistance to UAV-induced vibrational stresses. First hypothesis of medical payload container geometry and materials, implemented in the experimental campaign.
    \item \textbf{Fiorentino (2021)} \cite{Fiorentino2021}: development of a path-planning strategy for life-saving drug distribution in populated environments. Generation of risk maps and implementation of a rapidly-exploring random tree (RRT)-based algorithm to minimise global risk and flight time.
    \item \textbf{Angelini (2023)} \cite{Angelini2023} and \textbf{Angelini et al. (2024)} \cite{Angelini2024ICAS}: comparative analysis of the safety and economic efficiency of emergency medical deliveries performed by car, drone, and vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft. Identification of optimal pharmaceutical delivery hub locations for each transport mode to maximise logistical performance.
\end{itemize}

The present thesis was carried out in collaboration with the three institutions mentioned above to advance the research effort, bridging the gap between their long-term objective and the results achieved so far. This required preliminary work aimed at formalising the project requirements at both a broad, high level and at a specific, first-step level. Having taken a legislative, logistic, market- and mission-evaluation approach until now, it was possible to delineate a scenario in which the approval of ENAC could potentially be obtained to enable this type of operation. However, as certifications are unavoidably specific to mission type, trajectory and vehicle, it appeared necessary to implement a more concrete approach rooted in \textit{design}, whilst also adopting a \textit{safety-first} approach that diverted the project towards a device for the \textit{safety of the medical payload} rather, than that of the aerial vehicle. The deliberate prioritisation of this aspect is justified in \cref{section:market-analysis,section:payload-first-approach}, where it is illustrated on the basis of a regional market analysis and the exploration of an ongoing paradigm shift in the design process of biomedical devices.


\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{section:stakeholders}
Overall, the stakeholders participating in the present project had the following roles:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TU Delft} (Prof. Bianca Giovanardi): primary academic supervisor overseeing the scientific quality and methodological development of the research.
    \item \textbf{Politecnico di Torino} (Prof. Enrico Cestino, Davide Angelini): institutional leads and co-supervisors for the broader UAV medical-delivery research project, providing access to the experimental platforms used.
    \item \textbf{A.O.U. Città della Salute e della Scienza di Torino / Molinette Hospital}: provider of medical and operational data related to pharmaceutical transport requirements and mission scenarios.
    \item \textbf{FEV Italia}: industrial partner offering technical support for advanced modelling and simulation activities, including SolidWorks-based design and LS-DYNA impact analyses.
\end{itemize}

The interaction between stakeholders, as well as the management and communication aspect of the project, is analysed in \cref{chapter:projectManagement} - "Project Management".



\section{Document outline}
This thesis document is structured as follows: \Cref{chapter:SoA-and-RnD} gathers the necessary information from both a market analysis and a literature review, highlighting the gap between current research and development efforts and the long-term goal of establishing a UAV-based medical delivery operation over regional territories, and leads to the formulation of the research questions. \Cref{chapter:methodology} proposes a \textit{safety-first} design workflow to answer the research questions, and \cref{chapter:results} applies it to an exemplifying mission and payload, presenting the concrete results of this thesis. \Cref{chapter:conclusions} answers the research questions and gathers the findings into recommendations for future work, and \cref{chapter:projectManagement} discusses the project management aspects of the thesis.
